Tone & Guardrails

Rules that keep the system coherent across product, marketing, and community contexts.

✓
Do
  • Use Figma Sans for all new product and marketing surfaces.
  • Apply Inter only as a fallback where Figma Sans is unavailable — legacy plugin UIs, third-party integrations.
  • Keep backgrounds neutral (#FFFFFF, #F5F5F5, or dark equivalents) and let illustrations carry color.
  • Use purple (#A259FF) exclusively as a brand accent — never for informational states (use blue #0D99FF for info).
  • Use semantic tokens (--figma-color-bg-brand, --figma-color-text) rather than raw hex in plugin code.
  • Ensure minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio between text and its background at all times.
  • Apply shadow-focus (blue ring) on interactive elements for keyboard accessibility.
  • Use #F24822 consistently for all destructive and error states — never orange or lighter red.
  • Keep motion fast: 100–200 ms micro-interactions, 200–300 ms panel transitions, ease-out curves.
  • Maintain app information density — professionals expect tight UI.
✕
Don't
  • Don't use brand purple (#A259FF) or blue (#0D99FF) as full-surface backgrounds on large areas — accents only.
  • Don't mix Figma Sans with other grotesque sans-serifs (Helvetica, Arial, DM Sans) — creates tonal conflict.
  • Don't use decorative shadows on app UI components; reserve shadow-md and above for floating surfaces.
  • Don't use full black #000000 as text in the app — use #000000E5 (90% opacity) to soften retina rendering.
  • Don't produce hard corners (radius 0) for interactive components on the marketing site.
  • Don't use the Figma logo, wordmark, or brand marks inside app UI components you ship.
  • Don't rely on color alone to communicate state — always pair with an icon, label, or shape change.
  • Don't exceed 6 colors in a single component or layout section.
  • Don't animate the canvas itself — only chrome elements should animate.
  • Don't apply Figma Sans Mono to anything other than code, coordinates, hex values, or token names.
